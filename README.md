## Objective
Analyze a real-world online retail transaction dataset and communicate measurable findings and practical business recommendations.

## Dataset
UCI Machine Learning Repository — Online Retail:  
https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/352/online-retail

The source workbook contains 541,909 rows and eight original columns. The period is 2010-12-01 to 2011-12-09. The data is licensed under CC BY 4.0; retain attribution to the UCI repository.

## Key results
- Positive-sales revenue after exact duplicate removal and transaction filters: **£10,642,110.80**
- Unique invoices in the analysis: **19,960**
- Identified customers: **4,338**
- Average order value: **£533.17**
- Countries represented in filtered sales: **38**
- United Kingdom share of revenue: **84.6%**
- Highest-revenue month: **2011-11 (£1,503,866.78)**

## Method
1. Read the original Excel workbook.
2. Count missing values, exact duplicate rows, cancellation invoices and non-positive transaction values.
3. Remove exact duplicate rows.
4. For sales KPIs, exclude invoices beginning with `C`, rows with quantity <= 0, and rows with unit price <= 0.
5. Calculate line revenue as `Quantity × UnitPrice` in GBP.
6. Aggregate sales by month, country and product description.

Returns and cancellations are not silently treated as positive sales. This report's sales KPI is a filtered positive-sales measure, not a full accounting net-revenue figure.

## Limitations
The dataset covers a historic period (2010–2011), is heavily weighted toward the UK, and has no product cost or operating-expense fields. Therefore, revenue is not profit and the analysis cannot estimate margins. Customer-level analysis excludes rows without a CustomerID.
